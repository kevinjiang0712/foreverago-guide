Forever Ago chapter 3 photos center on the Highway Star Diner and its motel surroundings. The required dog photograph is behind the diner: speak to Maddy about the restricted booth, leave the restaurant, turn right through the fence, investigate the rubbish area, and photograph the dog. Return to Maddy, use the booth, and take the prompted picture through the window to continue the story.

Two optional petal memories also have recognizable landmarks here. <ContentLink id="forever-ago-memory-locations">The Morning After</ContentLink> uses the motel swimming-pool area, while <ContentLink id="forever-ago-petal-locations">Seeking Shelter</ContentLink> uses the diner terrace stairs and entrance. Those memory photographs are separate from the dog photograph and the main-story window shot.

## Keep the three photography tasks separate

| Photo task | Where to start | What you need to do | What comes next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Morning After | Motel pool area | Inspect the local petals and find the gathering point | Reveal the optional memory with the camera |
| Seeking Shelter | Diner terrace stairs and glass entrance | Finish the named petal cluster | Find the gathered petals and photograph the memory |
| Maddy's dog request | Counter after checking the booth | Investigate the rubbish area and photograph the animal | Report back to Maddy for booth access |
| Booth-window scene | Restricted booth after gaining access | Sit and take the prompted picture outside | Enter the playable story memory |
| Personal photographs | Accessible diner and motel spaces | Capture subjects you want to record | Review available pictures at the journal opportunity |

The important distinction is the result. A petal cluster unlocks a vignette, the dog picture answers Maddy's request, and the window photograph changes the story scene. Completing one does not automatically complete the others, even though you use the same camera for all of them.

## Search The Morning After around the pool

Look around the motel swimming pool rather than only on the main path toward the diner. The Morning After has a two-petal counter. One pictured location is by the raised area and steps around the pool, near the striped umbrella. The other is beside the pool slide and green fence.

<Media id="body-images-forever-ago-chapter-3-photos-pool" />

The pool rules board, slide, and fence help you recognize the correct pocket. Search the reachable paving beside those objects and inspect the petals when the interaction appears. These are close environmental checks. You do not need to jump into the water or find a different swimming facility elsewhere along the highway.

When the vignette unlocks, follow the instruction to find where the petals gather. The counter and the unlock message describe different stages. Finishing the two local pickups opens the memory opportunity; you still need to enter the gathering point and complete its camera interaction.

If you found one petal and lost the second, keep the pool area as your anchor. Check the steps and umbrella side, then the slide and fence side. This gives you a bounded sweep instead of repeatedly walking between the motel and every entrance of the diner.

Do this while the motel surroundings are available to explore. It is a separate optional memory, so do not wait for the dog request to direct you toward it. Maddy's task concerns the rubbish behind the restaurant, which is a different part of the chapter.

## Search Seeking Shelter at the terrace and door

Seeking Shelter belongs to the diner approach. Inspect the petals around the exterior terrace stairs, then the entrance area. The pictured interior landmark is the glass doorway beneath the exit sign, beside the membership sign and the yellow floor sign. Its local counter is two petals.

<Media id="body-images-forever-ago-chapter-3-photos-entrance" />

A useful route starts on the stairs and ends at the door. Look near the walking surface rather than at the tabletop or the distant landscape beyond the terrace railing. The small petals can be easy to overlook when your view is centered on the restaurant facade.

Once the vignette unlocks, locate the gathered petals and photograph the revealed memory. Do not mistake opening the restaurant door for finishing the vignette. The doorway is a location landmark; the petal and camera responses are the actual stages of the memory interaction.

You can complete this local search while moving between outdoor exploration and the restaurant interior. Keep the named response in mind so that you do not combine it with The Morning After. Both clusters contain two petals, but the shared count does not mean they are one task spread over the entire diner grounds.

If you are checking several optional subjects, mark the memory as photographed only after completing that action. A general exterior image of the neon diner or terrace can make a good personal picture, but it does not establish that the petal memory has been revealed.

## Complete the diner prerequisites before the booth photo

Alfred's diner visit involves flowers, a jukebox, and the booth. Leave the starting room and head toward the restaurant. Turn toward the van and pass through the nearby fence to reach the telescope area. Pick the flowers there for Audrey.

The flowers are a story pickup. They are not the loose petal clues for the two optional memories described above. If your task asks for Audrey's flowers, use the flower-picking interaction. Taking a close-up photograph of the patch does not collect the requested flowers.

Inside the diner, head left to the jukebox and choose Green Green Grass of Home by Patti Page. Then inspect the booth farther inside. Alfred discovers that using it requires membership privileges, which sends you to the counter to speak with Maddy.

These actions explain why a photograph through the window may not be available immediately on entering the restaurant. The booth has an access step. Resolve Maddy's request rather than photographing every window from the public seating area and expecting one of those shots to trigger the memory.

If the booth remains unavailable after you have explored outside, check the conversation with Maddy. Petal searches, flower picking, and a personal dog photograph taken without following the request are separate actions. The clear story route is to hear the favor, investigate, photograph the animal, and report the result.

## Find the dog behind the restaurant

After speaking with Maddy, leave the diner and turn right. Go through the nearby fence and continue toward the rubbish area. Investigate the hole and trash before raising the camera to take the dog's picture.

<Media id="body-images-forever-ago-chapter-3-photos-dog" />

The dog is tucked beside the rubbish bins and fence. Aim at the animal itself, not merely at the litter that identifies the area. A wide picture of the rubbish can document the problem while leaving the requested subject outside the view.

The route is short enough to use the diner entrance as your reference point. Return to the restaurant door if you become disoriented, face outward, and take the right-side fence route again. Avoid searching around the telescope for this task: that earlier route belongs to the flowers.

If the scene does not respond to your picture, lower the camera and check whether you completed the investigation interaction. Then reframe the dog with a clear view. You are looking for the animal identified by the request, so several pictures of a bin, fence, or hole will not improve the result.

Once the dog picture is taken, return inside and speak to Maddy. The report is a necessary part of the route to the booth. Do not stay outside waiting for the photograph itself to move Alfred automatically into the reserved seat.

## Take the booth-window photograph

With permission obtained, return to the booth and sit down. This is also an opportunity to handle available photographs in the journal. When the camera prompt appears, aim at the scenery visible through the window and take the picture.

<Media id="body-images-forever-ago-chapter-3-photos-window-photo" />

The booth provides the required position for this photograph. It is not simply an instruction to take a landscape picture from any point around the diner. If you are standing beside another window, return to the permitted booth and follow its seated sequence.

After the picture, the story moves into Alfred and Audrey's past. Follow the gate-sign interaction, meet Audrey, and continue the scene as directed. The chapter has moved beyond the current-day diner photo route, so it is normal for the previous exploration state to be replaced by the memory sequence.

You do not need to reproduce the picture as a jigsaw or align separate printed fragments. The useful solution is the subject and position: sit at the accessible booth, use the prompted camera, and photograph the exterior view. The scene itself handles the transition into the past.

## Check the chapter by the response you received

If a local petal counter is incomplete, return to that memory's landmark area. If the dog request is unresolved, inspect the rubbish area, take the animal's photograph, and speak to Maddy. If you have booth permission but the main memory has not started, sit and complete the prompted window picture.

Use the response to tell you which task succeeded. A vignette unlock message belongs to the petal search. Maddy's permission belongs to the dog request. A transition to the past belongs to the booth photograph. Those cues are more useful than counting how many pictures you have taken across the chapter.

Before leaving the present-day diner sequence, finish the optional motel and terrace searches you intend to pursue and any personal photographs you want to keep. Then complete the booth sequence. This order gives each activity a clear endpoint and prevents a scenic photograph from being mistaken for the specific picture the story is waiting for.
